About DailyFoodFighter

DailyFoodFighter is an independent digital publication dedicated to exploring the biochemical properties, nutritional density, and physiological mechanisms of natural foods through peer-reviewed scientific literature.

🎯 Our Mission

Amidst a landscape inundated with unsupported dietary fads and unverified health claims, DailyFoodFighter delivers accessible, evidence-based nutritional insights. Our analyses synthesize empirical findings from the USDA FoodData Central, national food composition databases, and respected international nutrition journals.

🔍 Editorial Scope

  • Fruits: Seasonal phytochemical profiles, antioxidant capacity, vitamin kinetics, and glycemic considerations.
  • Vegetables: Micronutrient bioavailability, dietary fiber fractions, and heat-induced bioaccessibility.
  • Meat & Protein: Essential amino acid scores, bioavailable heme iron, and lipid profiles.
  • Seafood: Marine omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (EPA/DHA), trace minerals, and culinary preparation.

Editorial approach and limitations

DailyFoodFighter is an independent food information site. AI may assist with drafting and summarizing content. Publication does not imply that every article has been individually reviewed by a physician or registered dietitian.

Nutrition figures should be read with their serving basis, edible portion, raw or cooked state and source in mind. Korean and US food composition data may differ by variety and analytical method. Study outcomes depend on the population, preparation and study design; they do not establish guaranteed prevention or treatment of disease.

If you find a possible error, please send the article URL and supporting evidence through our correction contact page.

How sources are cited

Every nutrition table states the basis of its values (raw, cooked, dried, canned) next to the source. US figures carry a USDA FoodData Central entry number (FDC ID) and a direct link; Korean figures name the Rural Development Administration's national standard food composition table as the basis and add its food code where the code has been verified. When a table follows the Korean table, the matching USDA entry and its FDC ID are listed beside it so the reader can see which value comes from which source. Chicken breast, for example, is shown as FDC 171077 (120 kcal, 22.5 g protein) beside Korean code I008000D020a (106 kcal, 22.97 g), with the sample and trimming difference explained rather than one value deleted.

When two databases disagree, both stay on the page. All 55 foods, their per-100 g values and their source IDs are listed in the nutrition calculator, and every row there was checked against the published dataset on 2026-11-26.
